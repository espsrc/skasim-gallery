Science case #1

MIGHTEE deep field

The COSMOS field of the MeerKAT International GHz Tiered Extragalactic Exploration (MIGHTEE), simulated at L-band for 4 h each with MeerKAT, with SKA-MID-AA* of the Square Kilometre Array (SKA), and with the Karl G. Jansky Very Large Array (VLA) in B and D configurations. On the same sky and in the same time, AA* reaches 1.78 µJy/beam at 1.5″ resolution, three times deeper than MeerKAT at a beam four times wider (robust −1 for both). Every image lands within 8% of the noise predicted from the radiometer equation, the image weighting and source confusion.

  • MeerKAT · SKA-MID-AA* · VLA B/D

Science motivation

MIGHTEE is the deep extragalactic continuum survey carried out with MeerKAT in South Africa (Jarvis et al. 2016). Its first data release reaches a thermal noise of 2.2 µJy/beam at 5.2″ in COSMOS, from a 22-pointing mosaic totalling 139.6 h on source (Hale et al. 2025). Observing the same patch of sky with SKA-Mid, and with the VLA as a northern reference, shows what the extra dishes and the 108 km baselines of AA* buy at matched integration time.

  • Compare the image rms and angular resolution of four arrays after 4 h on the same field.
  • Check that the simulated noise matches the radiometer equation once weighting and confusion are accounted for.
  • See where the image stops being thermal-noise limited and becomes confusion-limited.

Simulation runs

Four runs share the sky model, the phase centre and a 0.2° image, and differ only in the array, its band and the weighting. Each observes for 4 h in 8 s integrations, with thermal noise injected per baseline from the antenna system equivalent flux densities (SEFDs) and no RFI flagging. Images are made with WSClean (multiscale, 8 output channels joined into a multi-frequency image).

Sky model

The sky combines the 487 MIGHTEE DR1 components that fall in the 0.2° image (6.2 µJy to 1.80 mJy, with their deconvolved sizes; Hale et al. 2025) with 9632 sources from the Tiered Radio Extragalactic Continuum Simulation (T-RECS; Bonaldi et al. 2019) below the MIGHTEE completeness limit, between 0.2 and 24 µJy, which set the confusion floor. All components have a flat spectrum.

Takeaways

  • MeerKAT reaches 5.63 µJy/beam with a 5.9″ × 4.1″ beam at robust −1 in 4 h. Its dense core carries most of the natural weight, so robust −1 costs a factor 4.3 in noise over natural weighting.
  • SKA-MID-AA* reaches 1.78 µJy/beam with a 1.5″ × 0.9″ beam at the same robust and time: three times deeper than MeerKAT at a quarter of the beam width, from its 144 dishes and 108 km baselines.
  • VLA B reaches 4.58 µJy/beam with a 3.8″ × 2.9″ beam at robust 0, slightly deeper than MeerKAT at robust −1 with less than half the dishes.
  • VLA D produces a 40.0″ × 29.1″ beam and is confusion-limited: its 28.8 µJy/beam is 5.6 times its thermal noise, set by the blended faint population.
  • All runs share the same sky model and phase centre, so differences come from the array, its uv coverage and the weighting. Every image lands within 8% of the noise predicted from the radiometer equation, the weighting and confusion.
